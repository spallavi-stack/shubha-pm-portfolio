# Visitor analytics and funnel plan

Written August 2026. A plan for measuring who visits the portfolio and how far into it they get, given the site as it stands today: static HTML on GitHub Pages, no server, no build step beyond `scripts/build_docs.py`.

Nothing is instrumented right now. There is no analytics script of any kind on any page, so today the only visitor signal available is GitHub's repo traffic tab, which counts clones and repo views rather than site visits.

---

## 1. What the site gives you to work with

36 HTML pages, and the split between them decides most of the implementation cost:

| Group | Count | How they're made | Instrumentation cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project doc pages | 25 | Generated by `scripts/build_docs.py` from one `TEMPLATE` string | One line in the template, then rebuild |
| Hub and contact | 2 | Hand-written (`index.html`, `contact.html`) | Hand-edit |
| Case study pages | 3 | Hand-written (`flexy`, `sunnysideup`, `fund-the-future`) | Hand-edit |
| Prototypes | 4 | Hand-written (3 prototypes plus Flexy's utility dashboard) | Hand-edit |
| Calculator test harnesses | 2 | Hand-written, internal | Skip deliberately |

So the real work is nine hand-edited pages plus one template line, and that covers everything.

Three structural features make a funnel cheap to build here:

- **Paths already encode meaning.** `/projects/<slug>/index.html`, `/prototype.html`, `/docs/<name>.html`. A script can derive project and page type from `location.pathname` with no per-page configuration. This is the single most important design decision below.
- **Sections already have IDs.** `#tldr`, `#problem`, `#research`, `#roadmap`, `#prototype`, `#decisions`, `#limitations` on the case study pages, `#case-studies` and `#pm-lab` on the hub. Section-level depth measurement needs no new markup.
- **Four pages already load a shared script** (`assets/js/motion.js`), so there is an established pattern for a site-wide JS file.

## 2. The constraint that shapes everything: volume

A portfolio gets tens to low hundreds of sessions a month, and a share of those are you. A five-step funnel over 60 sessions leaves cells holding three or four people. Conversion rates computed on that are noise, and any decision made from a 12% versus 19% difference at that sample size is a decision made from nothing.

This does not make the project pointless. It changes what the dashboard should be built to answer.

**Questions this can answer honestly at low volume:**
- Does anyone reach the prototypes at all, or does the case study page absorb everyone?
- Which of the three case studies pulls the most opens, and which gets abandoned fastest?
- Do the source documents get opened by anyone, which bears directly on the open question of putting them behind a password (`portfolio-feedback.md`, item 6)?
- Does the PM Lab get touched now that it is collapsed behind a `<details>`?
- What does a single visitor's path through the site actually look like, end to end?
- Where do people arrive from, and does a LinkedIn visitor behave differently from a direct one?

**Questions it cannot answer for a long time:**
- Whether any change improved conversion. There will not be enough traffic to detect it.
- Anything requiring a statistically defensible comparison between two variants.

The practical implication: build for **reading individual journeys and counting absolute events**, and treat percentages as descriptive rather than as evidence. Prefer a tool that shows session-level paths over one that only shows aggregate rates.

## 3. The funnels worth defining

### Primary funnel: the hiring-manager journey

This is the one that matters, because it maps to the site's actual job.

1. **Land** on any page
2. **Reach the case study section**, either by scrolling to `#case-studies` on the hub or by landing directly on a case study page
3. **Open a case study**
4. **Engage with it**, meaning scroll past the halfway mark or open a source doc
5. **Open the prototype**
6. **Use the prototype**, meaning a real interaction rather than a load
7. **Show contact intent**: contact page, mailto click, LinkedIn click, or CV download

Steps 5 and 6 are the hinge. The prototypes are the strongest thing on the site and the feedback log already flags that the case studies bury them under text (`portfolio-feedback.md`, item 3). Measuring the drop between 4 and 5 puts a number on that complaint, and the agreed-but-unbuilt restructure has a before-and-after to point at.

### Secondary funnel: document depth

Case study page → doc opened → doc read to the end. Tells you whether the research documents are portfolio proof that people actually read or a wall nobody enters, which is the evidence the password question needs.

### Secondary funnel: PM Lab

Hub → `#pm-lab` section reached → `<details>` expanded → tool tab opened → tool actually used. The Lab is a large amount of built work now sitting collapsed. This tells you whether that was the right call.

### Secondary funnel: prototype internals

Prototype loaded → first interaction → completed the core action (SunnySideUp: a postcode entered and a verdict shown; Flexy: reaching the savings or rules screen; Fund the Future: reaching the campaign or give step). This is the closest the portfolio comes to product analytics on a product, and it is worth having for its own sake as a demonstrable artifact.

## 4. Event taxonomy

Name events once and keep them stable. Renaming later breaks historical comparison in every tool listed below.

Every event carries two properties derived automatically from the URL, so nothing has to be tagged by hand:

- `area`: `hub` | `case` | `prototype` | `doc` | `contact` | `dashboard`
- `project`: `flexy` | `sunnysideup` | `fund-the-future` | `none`

| Event | Extra properties | Fired by |
|---|---|---|
| `page_view` | `doc` (doc slug, when `area=doc`) | Automatic on load |
| `section_view` | `section` | IntersectionObserver over `section[id]`, once per section per session |
| `scroll_depth` | `pct` (25/50/75/100) | Scroll listener, once per threshold per page |
| `read_complete` | | Bottom of a doc page reached, plus a minimum dwell time |
| `case_open` | `from` (`hub_card`, `nav`, `direct`) | Delegated click on case card links |
| `doc_open` | `doc` | Delegated click on `docs/*.html` links |
| `prototype_open` | `from` | Delegated click on `prototype.html` links |
| `prototype_action` | `action`, `step` | Explicit calls inside each prototype |
| `pm_lab_expand` | | `<details id="pm-lab-details">` toggle |
| `pm_lab_tab` | `tool` | Delegated click on `.pm-lab-tab` |
| `pm_lab_action` | `tool`, `action` | Explicit calls in the tool scripts |
| `video_play` / `video_complete` | `video` | Media events, when the intro videos ship |
| `cv_download` | | Delegated click on the PDF link |
| `contact_click` | `channel` (`email`, `linkedin`) | Delegated click on the contact page |
| `outbound_click` | `host` | Delegated click on any external link |

Two rules keep this from rotting:

- **Nothing personally identifying, ever.** No names, no email addresses, no free-text field contents. The prototypes take postcode input, and that input must never be sent as an event property. Send `postcode_entered: true` and nothing more.
- **Explicit calls only where delegation cannot reach.** Everything driven by a link or a class gets caught by one delegated listener. Only in-prototype state changes need a line of code added at the call site.

## 5. Architecture

One file, `assets/js/analytics.js`, loaded with `defer` from every page. It does four things:

1. **Derives context** (`area`, `project`, `doc`) from `location.pathname`. No per-page config.
2. **Exposes one function**, `window.track(name, props)`, which is the only thing the rest of the site ever calls.
3. **Wires the automatic listeners**: delegated clicks, IntersectionObserver, scroll depth, `<details>` toggle, media events.
4. **Forwards to whichever vendor is configured**, behind a single adapter function at the bottom of the file.

That last point is the reason to write this file rather than pasting a vendor snippet into 36 pages. Switching tools, or running two in parallel during a migration, becomes a change to one function instead of a change to every page. It also means the vendor decision in section 6 does not block starting the work.

Two details worth building in from the start, both of which matter disproportionately at low volume:

- **Self-exclusion.** Visiting any page with `?notrack=1` sets a `localStorage` flag that suppresses all events from that browser permanently. Without this, your own development visits are a large fraction of the dataset.
- **A queue.** Buffer events fired before the vendor script finishes loading and flush them once it is ready, so early clicks are not silently dropped.

**Deployment mechanics:** add the `<script>` tag to `TEMPLATE` in `scripts/build_docs.py` and re-run it to cover all 25 doc pages, then hand-edit the nine remaining pages. The doc pages use relative paths at different depths, so the tag needs `../../../assets/js/analytics.js` in the template and `assets/js/analytics.js` at the root. Skip the two calculator test harnesses on purpose.

## 6. Choosing a tool

| | Cost | Funnels | Custom events | Consent banner needed | Session-level paths |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Umami Cloud** | Free tier | Yes | Yes | No, cookieless | Yes |
| **Plausible** | Business tier, around $19/mo | Business tier only | Yes | No, cookieless | Limited |
| **Google Analytics 4** | Free | Yes | Yes | Yes, in the EU | Limited without BigQuery |
| **GoatCounter** | Free | No | Basic | No | No |
| **Roll your own** | Hosting only | Whatever you build | Yes | No | Yes |

Sourcing note, following this repo's usual discipline: Umami's Hobby tier is listed as 100,000 events a month, 3 websites and 6 months of retention, with funnels, user journeys and session replay included, and Plausible gates funnels and its Stats API behind the Business plan at around $19 a month **(Fact, from vendor and review pages consulted August 2026, though Umami restructured its cloud plans in July 2026 and the pricing page renders client-side, so confirm the current tier at signup rather than trusting this table)**.

**Recommendation: Umami Cloud, on the free tier.**

The reasoning:

- Funnels are the actual requirement, and Umami includes them at no cost while Plausible charges around $19 a month for the same thing. At portfolio traffic, paying for funnels is hard to justify.
- Cookieless with no personal data collected means no consent banner. A cookie wall on a portfolio's landing page costs real visitors at the exact moment you have their attention, and GA4 in the EU brings one.
- Session-level journeys are the view that works at this traffic volume, per section 2.
- It is open source, so if the free tier changes you can self-host the same schema and keep the history.

Two limitations to accept knowingly:

- **Six months of retention on the free tier** means no year-over-year view. If the portfolio runs for years, either pay to extend or export monthly. Building a monthly export into the plan later is cheap insurance.
- **Ad blockers will eat some share of traffic.** Umami is a known-blocked domain in common filter lists, and the audience most likely to block is technically sophisticated people at tech companies, which is precisely your target audience. This undercounts, and it undercounts non-randomly.

**On GA4 specifically:** there is one legitimate reason to pick it that has nothing to do with data quality. "Instrumented and analysed a funnel in GA4" is a line a hiring manager recognises, and a screenshot of a GA4 funnel exploration is legible to people who have never heard of Umami. If that career signal matters more than the data quality and the consent banner, GA4 is a defensible choice. It is possible to run both, with the adapter in section 5 forwarding to each, at the cost of a consent banner and more moving parts.

**On the ad blocker problem:** the standard fix is serving the analytics script from your own domain so it looks first-party. That is not available on `spallavi-stack.github.io` because the domain is shared and you cannot proxy from it. Buying a custom domain and pointing GitHub Pages at it would fix this, and would also make the portfolio URL itself better. Worth considering as an adjacent decision rather than part of this one.

## 7. The dashboard

Three options, in increasing order of effort.

**A. Use Umami's own dashboard.** Zero build. Define the funnels from section 3 in its funnel builder, bookmark it, done. This is the right starting point and it may be the right ending point.

**B. Build a read-only dashboard page in the repo.** An `analytics.html` styled with the site's own design system, pulling numbers from Umami's API. The obstacle is that a static page cannot hold an API key without publishing it, so this needs a small proxy, for example a Cloudflare Worker that holds the key and returns only aggregates. Roughly half a day for the Worker and a day for the page.

The honest argument for B is that it is itself portfolio material. A PM who defined her own funnel, instrumented it, and built the dashboard she reads it in is demonstrating the exact loop the case studies describe. If it gets built, it should be a linked case study rather than a hidden page, and it should be candid about the sample sizes.

**C. Own the collection end too.** A Worker plus D1 or KV receiving the events directly, with no vendor. Maximum control and no ad blocker problem, since the endpoint is your own. It also means owning bot filtering, deduplication, and retention, which is where self-built analytics usually goes wrong. Only worth it if owning the raw event data is the point.

Recommendation: A now, B once there is enough data to make a dashboard worth looking at, C probably never.

## 8. What will skew the data

Worth writing down before you start reading numbers, so the first surprising chart does not get over-interpreted.

- **Your own visits.** Handled by the `?notrack=1` exclusion in section 5. Do this on day one.
- **Bots and link unfurlers.** LinkedIn, Slack and WhatsApp fetch pages to build preview cards, and crawlers hit GitHub Pages steadily. Client-side JS analytics filters most of this automatically because bots generally do not execute the script, which is a genuine argument for this approach over server log analysis, though GitHub Pages does not offer logs anyway.
- **Ad blockers**, per section 6. Assume the true numbers are higher than measured, by an unknown and non-uniform amount.
- **Direct traffic from applications.** A link in a job application arrives as direct traffic with no referrer, which is the highest-intent traffic on the site and the hardest to attribute. Fix this with UTM parameters on the links you send out yourself, for example `?utm_source=application&utm_campaign=<company>`. That single habit will probably teach you more than the funnel will in the first few months.
- **The `<details>` collapse on the PM Lab.** A section that starts collapsed cannot be compared against one that did not, so PM Lab numbers only mean something from the day the current layout shipped.

## 9. Build sequence

| Phase | Work | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Create the Umami account, add the site, confirm the free tier's current limits | 30 min |
| 2 | Write `assets/js/analytics.js`: context derivation, `track()`, the adapter, self-exclusion, queue | 2 to 3 hrs |
| 3 | Add the script tag to `TEMPLATE` in `build_docs.py`, rebuild, hand-edit the nine remaining pages, verify events arrive from each page type | 1 to 2 hrs |
| 4 | Add the automatic listeners: delegated clicks, section views, scroll depth, outbound, CV, contact | 2 hrs |
| 5 | Add explicit `track()` calls inside the three prototypes and the PM Lab tools | 2 to 3 hrs |
| 6 | Define the four funnels in Umami, add a privacy note to `contact.html` | 1 hr |
| 7 | Let it run. Read nothing for a month | |
| 8 | Optional: the custom dashboard, option B in section 7 | 1 to 2 days |

Phases 1 to 4 are the whole thing in practice. Phase 5 is what makes the prototype funnel real, and it is the part most likely to get skipped, so it is worth doing while the schema is fresh.

---

## 10. Decisions needed before phase 2

1. **Umami, or GA4 for the CV signal, or both.** Section 6 recommends Umami alone. This changes the adapter and whether a consent banner is needed, and nothing else.
2. **Custom domain, yes or no.** Fixes part of the ad blocker problem and improves the URL. Independent of everything else here, and it is easier to do before the analytics history starts than after.
3. **Is the dashboard a private tool or a case study.** If it is a case study, it should be built as one, and the site's own funnel becomes a documented portfolio artifact rather than a hidden page.
4. **How much prototype instrumentation.** Full step-by-step funnels inside all three prototypes, or a single "reached the core action" event each. The first is considerably more work and considerably more interesting.
