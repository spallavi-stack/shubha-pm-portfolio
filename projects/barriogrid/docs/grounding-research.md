# BarrioGrid — Grounding Research

## Regulatory framework: collective self-consumption and distribution coefficients

**[Fact]** Spain's collective self-consumption model is defined by Real Decreto 244/2019, and the mechanism for variable (as opposed to fixed) distribution coefficients was added by Orden TED/1247/2021, which modified RD 244/2019's Annex I. (Source: [BOE-A-2021-18706](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2021-18706))

**[Fact]** Under Orden TED/1247/2021, a group using variable hourly coefficients must submit a plain-text (`.txt`, not XML) file to the distributor containing all 8,760 hours of the year, one line per hour per consumer, with fields CUPS, hour number (1–8760), and coefficient (decimal), semicolon-separated, comma as decimal marker. The filename is `[CAU code]_[year].txt`. A fixed-coefficient group instead submits one coefficient per consumer, without the hourly breakdown. (Source: [BOE-A-2021-18706](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2021-18706))

**[Fact]** All participating consumers (or their appointed commercial representative) must submit an identical copy of this file, backed by a written agreement signed by every participant. Once coefficients are submitted, the distributor activates them on the 1st of the following month if filed on days 1–10, or the 1st of the month after that if filed on day 11 or later. Once active, coefficients can be changed no more than once every four months, except during an initial four-month grace period for a group moving from fixed to variable coefficients. (Source: [BOE-A-2021-18706](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2021-18706))

**[Fact]** The CAU (Código de Autoconsumo) is a 26-digit code identifying the installation and linking its generation and consumption points; for a collective installation it is derived from the CUPS of one participating consumer with `1FA000` appended. (Source: search-snippet summary of i-DE's public trámite documentation; the source PDF itself — i-DE's `guia_tramitacion_ac_colectivo_bt` — could not be parsed directly in this session, see Open Questions.)

**[Inference]** The scope document's framing of the bottleneck as "XML file generation" appears to be a naming mismatch rather than a missed requirement: the actual submission format specified in the governing order is a semicolon-delimited plain-text file, not XML. This matters for the technical-feasibility doc — the prototype should target generating a correctly-formatted `.txt` coefficient file per the BOE-published spec, and any XML terminology in earlier framing should be corrected rather than carried forward as a requirement.

**[Fact]** A 2024–2025 regulatory update raised the maximum distance between a generation installation and a participating consumer in shared self-consumption from 500 m to 5,000 m, and shortened minimum modality-change windows, specifically to support industrial-polygon and dispersed-urban shared self-consumption projects. (Source: [Xolary, "Regulación del Autoconsumo Compartido en 2025"](https://xolary.com/autoconsumo-compartido-2025/); corroborated by [Porcuna Digital, March 2026](https://www.porcunadigital.es/2026/03/el-nuevo-radio-de-5-kilometros-para-el.html))

**[Assumption]** The scope's anchor model (<2 km radius) sits comfortably inside both the old 500 m-plus rule's practical exceptions and the new 5,000 m ceiling, so BarrioGrid's model is assumed to be compliant on distance grounds without needing to rely on the newest rule change. This is a reasonable reading of the public sources found, not independently confirmed against the full current text of RD 244/2019 as amended.

**[Fact]** The EU-level framework enabling this kind of arrangement is RED II (Directive (EU) 2018/2001), which defines peer-to-peer trading as "the sale of renewable energy between market participants by means of a contract with pre-determined conditions governing the automated execution and settlement of the transaction, either directly between market participants or indirectly through a certified third-party market participant, such as an aggregator," alongside the Internal Electricity Market Directive (EU) 2019/944 and Regulation (EU) 2019/943. (Source: [Florence School of Regulation, "Peer-to-peer trading and energy communities"](https://fsr.eui.eu/peer-to-peer-trading-and-energy-communities/))

**[Fact]** Spain is characterized in EU-level literature as one of the more advanced member states in transposing energy-community and energy-sharing provisions, alongside Portugal, France, Italy and Austria. (Source: [Florence School of Regulation](https://fsr.eui.eu/peer-to-peer-trading-and-energy-communities/))

**[Inference]** Spain's actual implementation, however, still routes settlement through the distributor's coefficient mechanism rather than through a genuinely peer-to-peer automated contract layer as RED II's definition envisions. BarrioGrid's "matching" logic is therefore best understood as a pricing/allocation-recommendation layer sitting on top of the existing coefficient-based settlement system, not a replacement for it — the distributor still executes the physical/financial settlement via the coefficients BarrioGrid would need to help its members choose and file correctly.

## i-DE (Iberdrola distribution network) as the operational bottleneck

**[Fact]** i-DE publishes its own collective self-consumption trámite guide (`guia_tramitacion_ac_colectivo_bt`) and a dedicated landing page describing the collective self-consumption process. (Source: [i-DE, "Autoconsumo Colectivo"](https://www.i-de.es/novedades/detalle/autoconsumo-colectivo))

**[Assumption]** This session's environment could not extract readable text from i-DE's PDF trámite guide (the WebFetch attempt returned corrupted/binary content rather than parseable text) or from ESIOS's own API landing page (returned only a page title, no body content). Both are treated as inconclusive rather than as confirmation that no useful detail exists there — a follow-up pass with `pdftotext` (confirmed working in a prior project on this repo, per CLAUDE.md) against a locally-downloaded copy of the i-DE PDF is a concrete next step, not a dead end.

**[Fact]** Distribution-coefficient modifications, once a group has moved to the variable-coefficient regime, are gated at once per four months by the governing order itself (see above) — this is a rule set by BOE-published regulation, not by i-DE's own internal policy, so it applies regardless of which Spanish distributor a project sits under. (Source: [BOE-A-2021-18706](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2021-18706))

**[Inference]** The real technical bottleneck for BarrioGrid is therefore less "can we generate an accepted file" (the format is publicly specified and mechanical to produce) and more "can the coefficient values we compute ever be submitted often enough to track a genuinely dynamic 12¢/kWh arbitrage." A file submitted and activated only once every four months cannot reflect hour-by-hour or even week-by-week shifts in solar output or consumer demand mix. This is a material product-design constraint, not just an integration detail, and should be treated as load-bearing in the technical feasibility doc and the product brief's Open Questions.

**[Assumption]** It is assumed (not directly confirmed in this pass) that i-DE's submission channel and validation rules do not diverge materially from the BOE-published spec, since the order is a national regulation binding on all distributors rather than i-DE-specific guidance. This should be checked against i-DE's own trámite guide directly once it can be parsed.

## Competitive grounding: Batería Virtual (Virtual Battery) offers

**[Fact]** A "batería virtual" in the Spanish market is not a physical storage device but an accounting service offered by a retailer/commercializadora: unconsumed surplus-compensation value is banked as a credit balance usable against future bill line items (power term, taxes), rather than paid out or physically redirected to another consumer. (Source: [1KOMMA5°, "Excedentes de placas solares... y Batería Virtual (2025)"](https://1komma5.com/es/blog/placas-solares/compensacion-por-excedentes-autoconsumo/))

**[Fact]** Surplus compensation rates offered by retailers running these programs typically fall in the €0.04–0.10/kWh range as of 2025, moving toward €0.05–0.12/kWh for 2026 depending on the company and contract; Iberdrola's own "Plan Solar" is cited at €0.06/kWh. Repsol's Vivit virtual-battery service is cited at an added flat fee of €1.99/month. (Source: [alromar-energia.es, "Las 5 mejores Baterías Virtuales y Comparativa 2026"](https://alromar-energia.es/blog/comparativa-de-baterias-virtuales/))

**[Fact]** Many virtual-battery programs reset the accumulated credit balance annually (commonly each June or December), meaning banked summer surplus can be lost before a household would use it to offset winter consumption. (Source: [alromar-energia.es](https://alromar-energia.es/blog/comparativa-de-baterias-virtuales/))

**[Fact]** Within shared/collective self-consumption specifically (i.e. the same regulatory mechanism BarrioGrid would use), surplus is distributed among neighbors by coefficient, and the maximum distance for shared installations was 500 m before the 2024–2025 rule change discussed above. (Source: [SunFields, "Autoconsumo colectivo compartido"](https://www.sfe-solar.com/autoconsumo/colectivo/))

**[Inference]** The concrete gap between Batería Virtual offers and BarrioGrid's proposed model is not "one exists and one doesn't" — collective self-consumption coefficient-sharing already exists as a mechanism — but that Batería Virtual programs price the prosumer's surplus at the retailer's own banked-credit rate (~€0.04–0.10/kWh, often with an annual reset/expiry that reduces its effective value further) and settle it as an accounting credit against the *same* retailer's bill, not as a directly negotiated inter-consumer rate. BarrioGrid's ~€0.12/kWh matched rate is a real-money rate between two independent parties, paid without the annual-reset risk that erodes the economic value of a virtual-battery credit balance.

**[Assumption]** No source found in this pass documents a Batería Virtual program that also handles the distribution-coefficient submission and dynamic reallocation across a specific named group of neighbors as a service — the retailer programs found are structured around a single consumer's own surplus and their own bill, not a multi-party coefficient-managed arrangement. This is stated as an assumption because it reflects an absence in the sources reviewed, not a confirmed claim that no such combined offering exists anywhere in the market.

## Market sizing

**[Fact]** As of June 2023, the Valencian Community (Comunidad Valenciana) had surpassed 63,300 self-consumption installations totaling over 692 MW of installed capacity, up from fewer than 500 installations and about 5 MW in 2019. (Source: [El Salto, "Las renovables valencianas más allá de las grandes instalaciones"](https://www.elsaltodiario.com/valencia/renovables-valencianas-grandes-instalaciones))

**[Fact]** The Valencian Community has an estimated 51.7 million m² of available space across industrial polygons and undeveloped urban land, assessed as capable of reaching 2,590 MW of installed capacity — described as 43% of the EU's 2030 renewable-capacity target allocated to Valencian territory. (Source: [SunFields / regional press aggregation citing regional government figures](https://www.sfe-solar.com/subvenciones-placas-solares/comunidad-valenciana/))

**[Inference]** Even a small fraction of that 51.7 million m² industrial-rooftop inventory converting to collective self-consumption arrangements at the 100–150 kW anchor scale named in BarrioGrid's model would represent a large number of addressable "1 prosumer + 15–20 consumers" clusters across the region — this is a reasoned extrapolation from the industrial-land figure, not a directly published count of eligible sites, and should be treated as directional sizing rather than a precise addressable-market number.

**[Fact]** The 2024–2025 rule change raising the shared-installation radius from 500 m to 5,000 m was explicitly framed in coverage as targeted at industrial polygons and dispersed urban/suburban environments — i.e., regulatory intent is aligned with, not incidental to, exactly the industrial-anchor-plus-nearby-residential structure BarrioGrid proposes. (Source: [Porcuna Digital, March 2026](https://www.porcunadigital.es/2026/03/el-nuevo-radio-de-5-kilometros-para-el.html))

**[Assumption]** No comparable-market analog with a multi-year track record of this exact hyperlocal industrial-to-residential P2P arbitrage model (as opposed to generic collective self-consumption or virtual-battery banking) was found in this pass, in Spain or elsewhere in the EU. Academic literature on P2P energy trading (e.g. arXiv preprints on transactive-energy coalition formation) treats this largely as a modeling/mechanism-design problem rather than documenting a live deployed market, which is itself informative: this suggests BarrioGrid's specific matched-rate, cross-party model is closer to a novel offering within the existing regulatory envelope than an established product category with comparable-market precedent. Flag this for the product brief: market sizing rests on addressable industrial-rooftop inventory and regulatory-intent alignment, not on an existing comparable market's growth curve.

## Runtime input map for the arbitrage-matching calculation

BarrioGrid's core calculation needs, at minimum: the prosumer's real-time/near-real-time generation profile, each consumer's real-time/near-real-time consumption profile, a reference wholesale or retail price signal to justify the matched rate against, and the distributor's currently-active coefficient allocation (since that, not BarrioGrid's own logic, is what physically settles the trade).

**[Fact]** PVGIS (Photovoltaic Geographical Information System), maintained by the EU Joint Research Centre, is a free, registration-free public tool and API providing location-specific solar radiation and PV generation-potential estimates, with both a web API and a documented CLI/core API for automated access. (Source: [PVGIS documentation](https://pvgis.pages.code.europa.eu/pvgis/), [JRC PVGIS overview](https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/pvgis-online-tool_en))

**[Inference]** PVGIS is well-suited to backtesting the prosumer's *expected* generation profile (a modeled, weather-driven estimate for the Paterna/Torrent location and the stated 100–150 kW system), but it is a geographic/typical-year model, not the prosumer's own metered output — it cannot substitute for real metered generation data in a live product, only in a backtest.

**[Fact]** Datadis is the Spanish distribution companies' joint platform giving consumers free access to their own metered electricity consumption/generation data, and supports third-party authorization: a data owner can authorize a named third party (identified by NIF) to access specific CUPS (supply points) for a given validity window via the platform's own authorization flow. (Source: [Iberdrola España, "Nace DATADIS"](https://www.iberdrolaespana.com/en/sala-comunicacion/noticias/detalle/200730_np_02_datadis); authorization-flow detail from search-snippet summary of Datadis support documentation)

**[Assumption]** This session's direct fetch of Datadis's own developer FAQ (`dev.datadis.es/faqs`) returned an HTTP 403, so the exact technical detail on API v1 vs v2 endpoint differences, data granularity (e.g. hourly vs quarter-hourly), and historical depth of available consumption data is not independently confirmed in this pass — treated as an open item pending a retry or an alternative source (e.g. the third-party `ctr-datadis` Python SDK's own documentation, which does describe using both API versions).

**[Fact]** ESIOS, Red Eléctrica's public data/transparency platform, provides day-ahead market price and PVPC-related data via a free API, gated by a personal access token requested by email (`consultasios@ree.es`, subject "Personal token request"), typically issued within 24 hours at no cost, with no stated expiry on the token itself. (Source: [Energía Chi-cuadrado, "Cómo conseguir datos de ESIOS con su API"](https://www.energychisquared.com/post/c%C3%B3mo-conseguir-datos-de-esios-con-su-api/); [ESIOS API access guide PDF](https://api.esios.ree.es/documents/244/download?locale=en))

**[Inference]** ESIOS is well-placed to supply the wholesale/day-ahead price reference needed to justify BarrioGrid's matched rate as sitting rationally between the utility's buy and sell prices, but its token-request-by-email process (not instant self-service signup) is a manual step that should be listed explicitly as a setup dependency in the technical feasibility doc, not assumed to be instantly available.

**[Assumption]** No API source found in this pass directly supplies the currently-active distribution coefficients for a given CAU — that data appears to live inside the distributor's own coefficient-submission/activation workflow (i-DE, per the BOE-specified `.txt` file mechanism above) rather than being exposed through Datadis, ESIOS, or PVGIS. This is a genuine gap in the runtime input map: BarrioGrid's prototype can compute a recommended coefficient allocation from PVGIS/Datadis/ESIOS data, but confirming what the distributor is *actually currently applying* would require either manual cross-checking against billing data or i-DE-specific access not covered by the three named open APIs. This should be named explicitly in the technical feasibility doc's Open Questions.

- **i-DE's own trámite PDF could not be parsed in this session** (binary/corrupted content on fetch). The CAU-code structure and any i-DE-specific procedural detail beyond the BOE-published national spec rests on a search-snippet summary, not a direct primary-source read. - **Datadis's own developer documentation (`dev.datadis.es`) returned HTTP 403** on direct fetch in this session. API granularity, historical depth, and v1/v2 endpoint differences are not independently confirmed — flagged as the single lowest-confidence item in the runtime input map, since the prototype's backtesting design depends on knowing exactly what Datadis can return.
- **No confirmed API or data source exists for the distributor's currently-active coefficient values.** This is a structural gap, not a research-completeness gap — it likely needs to be named as an explicit open question in the product brief and addressed in the technical feasibility doc's scope of what the prototype can and cannot verify against ground truth.
- **The regulatory framing of the technical bottleneck should be corrected from "XML" to the BOE-specified plain-text/semicolon-delimited format** before this feeds into the product brief or technical feasibility doc, since the two have materially different implications for what the prototype needs to generate.
- **The four-month coefficient-change cadence is a hard regulatory ceiling on how dynamic BarrioGrid's matching can actually be in production**, independent of any technical bottleneck — this is arguably the single most consequential finding in this document for the product's core value proposition and should be surfaced prominently, not buried, in the product brief.
