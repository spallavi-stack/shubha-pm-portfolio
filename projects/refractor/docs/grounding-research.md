# Refractor — Grounding Research

Internal backing material. Not portfolio-facing, not rendered to HTML, not linked from the site. The product brief will compress this. This file stays as the full sourcing and confidence record.

Every claim is tagged **Fact** (cited to a real source), **Inference** (reasoned from stated facts, reasoning shown), or **Assumption** (unverified, flagged).

## Status

Second pass, 20 August 2026. Covers the regulatory terrain across Europe, the eco-modulation finding that underpins the commercial argument, a first competitive read, and the three product questions this pass was asked to settle: what the USP is, when the product gets used, and how a recommendation turns into an actual swap.

Not yet covered: pricing and willingness to pay (deferred by decision, monetization is an open question rather than a locked model), supplier data availability at the individual component level, and persona work.

**The product this pass describes.** Refractor asks a small cosmetics brand what it is putting on shelf and what that product is packaged in, then recommends packaging material substitutions that reduce carbon without changing cost or timeline, and connects the brand to suppliers who can make the substitution. Formula is out of scope. The brand is not charged.

## 1. The regulatory terrain

### 1.1 The product layer is harmonised

**Fact.** Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 Article 11 requires the Responsible Person to keep a Product Information File for ten years after the last batch was placed on the market, containing a description of the product, the Cosmetic Product Safety Report per Article 10(1), a description of the manufacturing method with a statement of GMP compliance per Article 8, proof of claimed effect where justified, and data on animal testing ([EUR-Lex, Regulation 1223/2009](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32009R1223)).

**Fact.** Article 10 requires a safety assessment before market placement. Annex I Part B requires the assessor to hold a university qualification in pharmacy, toxicology, medicine or a similar discipline, or a course recognised as equivalent by a member state ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32009R1223)).

**Fact.** CPNP notification runs through one EU portal covering all 27 member states and can only be filed by the Responsible Person. The applicable GMP standard is EN ISO 22716:2007 ([Ecosistant](https://www.ecosistant.eu/en/the-responsible-person-role-under-the-cosmetics-regulation-1223-2009/), [Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/cosmetic-regulations/eu-cosmetics-regulation/cpnp/)).

**Inference.** A brand does this work once and can then legally place the product anywhere in the EU. This is the layer brands plan and budget for, and it is not where Refractor operates.

### 1.2 The packaging layer is not harmonised

This is the finding that shapes the product.

**Fact.** Retail buyers ask cosmetics brands for six categories of documentation before approving a listing: product safety documentation (CPSR, PIF, toxicological evaluations), Responsible Person information, claims substantiation files, ingredient and formula documentation, testing and stability records, and packaging and labelling compliance review ([GCRS, 24 June 2026](https://www.gcrs.co.uk/the-compliance-documentation-retail-buyers-expect-before-approving-cosmetic-listings/)). Carbon footprint appears nowhere in that list. The only environmental foothold is "sustainable" as one claim type inside claims substantiation.

**Inference.** A product carbon footprint is not a gate on retail listing today. Refractor should not claim to clear one. Reasoning: the source is a regulatory consultancy with a commercial incentive to list every document a brand might need, so absence from a maximal list is stronger evidence than absence from a minimal one.

**Fact: language.** Labelling language is set nationally. Own national language only in Austria, Bulgaria, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Norway. Multiple languages in Belgium (Dutch, French, German), Finland (Finnish and Swedish) and Luxembourg (French, German, Luxembourgish). English accepted in Ireland, Cyprus and Malta. France (Toubon law, only "Made in" exempt) and Italy require full translation by national law ([Biorius](https://biorius.com/cosmetic-news/eu-cosmetic-language-requirements/)).

**Fact: packaging producer responsibility is per country.** There is no single EU packaging EPR registration. A producer registers, reports and pays in every member state where its packaging reaches a customer ([ppwrobligations.com](https://ppwrobligations.com/epr/), [ecocomply](https://ecocomply.ai/blog/packaging-epr-registration-country-by-country)).

**Fact: country-specific packaging marks.** France requires the Triman logo plus France-specific sorting instructions on all household packaging, mandatory since September 2022 ([Lizenzero](https://www.lizenzero.de/en/blog/labelling-requirements-in-france-the-triman-logo-and-sorting-instructions/)). Spain's Royal Decree 1055/2022 requires household packaging to indicate the disposal fraction ([Ecoveritas](https://www.ecoveritas.com/articles/mandatory-environmental-labelling-for-packaging-across-europe-what-you-need-to-know)). Cyprus is reported as the last country mandating the Green Dot logo.

**Fact: national establishment declarations.** France requires manufacturers and distributors established in France to declare, importers exempt, and DGCCRF took this over from ANSM on 1 January 2024 ([coslaw.eu](https://coslaw.eu/france-national-requirements-for-cosmetic-products/), [ANSM](https://ansm.sante.fr/vos-demarches/industriel/declarer-mon-etablissement)). Italy requires notification to the Ministry of Health and regional authority if manufacturing or packaging in Italy ([Lexora](https://lexoraeu.com/cosmetic-compliance-italy/)). Germany requires the Responsible Person to notify local authorities of importer status where Germany is the first EU country of import ([coslaw.eu](https://coslaw.eu/national-requirements-to-place-cosmetics-on-the-german-market/)).

**Fact: outside the EU.** The UK runs a complete parallel stack: separate UK Responsible Person, separate SCPN notification portal, UK RP name and address on the label since 31 December 2022, and its own packaging EPR. A brand in both markets runs two Responsible Persons and a dual-labelling strategy ([Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/news/the-uk-regulation-the-cosmetic-regulation-after-brexit/), [euverify](https://euverify.com/guides/how-to-comply-with-eu-uk-cosmetics-regulations/)). Norway is in the EEA so the cosmetics regulation applies, with Norwegian labelling. Switzerland and Liechtenstein were not researched in this pass.

**Inference.** The EU harmonised the product and left the packaging to member states. Every national divergence that costs a brand money is a packaging divergence: language on the pack, a national mark on the pack, a register that wants component materials and weights, and a fee priced per kilo per material. A brand entering five markets supplies the same component-level data five times in five formats.

### 1.3 PPWR sits on top of all of it

**Fact.** Regulation (EU) 2025/40 entered into force 11 February 2025 and became generally applicable **12 August 2026**. From that date no packaging may be placed on the EU market without a valid EU Declaration of Conformity, with no transition or sell-through period for existing stock. Conformity assessment under Article 38 and Annex VII, supporting technical documentation, and the DoC under Article 39 are mandatory ([Circularise](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/ppwr-guide-to-compliance-timelines-and-mass-balance-solutions), [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity), [Tanso](https://www.tanso.de/en/blog/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity-and-technical-documentation-what-companies-must-submit-from-august-12-2026)).

**Fact.** Anyone placing packaged goods on the market under their own brand, and determining the packaging design, is the manufacturer for these purposes and must sign the DoC, assuming full legal responsibility. The technical documentation behind it requires material and resin composition, recycled content percentages, empty-space ratios, and heavy metal documentation ([Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity), [Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/news/eu-ppwr-cosmetic-packaging-compliance/)).

**Fact.** Article 16 obliges suppliers to provide the manufacturer with all information and documentation necessary to demonstrate conformity, verified directly against the regulation text: "Suppliers of packaging or packaging materials provide the manufacturer with all the information and documentation necessary for the manufacturer to demonstrate the conformity of the packaging and the packaging materials" ([EUR-Lex, Regulation 2025/40](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202500040)).

**Fact.** From 1 January 2030, design-for-recycling compliance applies, plastic packaging must contain minimum 35% post-consumer recycled content, and packaging graded below E-level (under 70% recyclable) is prohibited ([Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/news/eu-ppwr-cosmetic-packaging-compliance/)).

**Fact.** Micro-enterprise relief is narrow. A business under 10 employees with turnover or balance sheet at or below €2 million is relieved of technical documentation and the DoC only where its packaging supplier is EU-based in the same member state, in which case the supplier assumes those obligations. No relief from EPR ([Packaging Hub](https://packaginghub.fr/en/articles/ppwr-micro-enterprises), [Compliance Gate](https://www.compliancegate.com/ppwr-micro-enterprises/)).

**Assumption, flagged.** Multiple secondary sources state that Article 44 requires registration in each member state's producer register, that Article 45 requires a non-established producer to appoint an Authorised Representative in each state where it makes packaging available (mandatory EU-wide from 12 August 2026), and that Article 68 requires member states to have penalty rules by 12 February 2027. EUR-Lex would not serve the PPWR full text on three separate URL forms during this pass, returning HTTP 202, so these three article references are **not** verified against primary law. Verify before publishing.

**Fact.** In practice the document is not the hard part. The Annex VIII template sits inside the regulation itself and third-party templates carry no independent legal weight. The work is collecting supplier evidence, which takes weeks, arrives in inconsistent formats, and is currently tracked in spreadsheets with columns for supplier, packaging type, status and date received ([Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity), [DPP-Tool](https://dpp-tool.com/en/blog/ppwr-declaration-conformity/)).

**Inference.** Article 16 removes the concern that packaging data might be unobtainable. A brand has a legal right to demand material composition, recycled content and weights from its suppliers. The remaining problem is knowing what to ask for and tracking who answered, which is a workflow problem rather than an access problem.

## 2. Eco-modulation: why a swap pays for itself

This is the strongest commercial finding in the pass and it is what makes a free tool viable for a brand with no sustainability budget.

**Fact.** EPR fees are modulated on environmental performance. Citeo fees run roughly €100 to €700 per tonne, with discounts for easily recyclable packaging and surcharges up to 50% higher for mixed-material or hard-to-sort packaging. CONAI has modulated its fee since 2018 for plastic and 2019 for paper, explicitly to promote higher reusability and recyclability ([CONAI](https://www.conai.org/en/companies/services-for-managing-consortium-obligations-and-business-opportunities/epr-fee/modulated-epr-fee-for-plastic-packaging/), [RegSurance](https://regsurance.com/eco-modulation-ppwr-epr-packaging-fees/)).

**Fact.** Under PPWR, eco-modulated fees tied to recyclability grades become mandatory in every national scheme, on bonus/malus logic. Packaging that is easier to collect, sort and recycle attracts lower fees. Packaging that is hard to recycle or relies heavily on virgin material faces higher fees ([RegSurance](https://regsurance.com/reduce-your-ppwr-epr-fees-in-2025-2030-eco-modulation-and-smart-packaging-strategies-to-cut-costs-by-20-50-for-businesses-in-europe/), [Tanso](https://www.tanso.de/en/blog/epr-under-the-ppwr-extended-producer-responsibility-modulated-fees-and-registration-in-the-lucid-register)).

**Inference.** A recommended swap has a financial return, not only an environmental one. Moving a component to recycled content or to a more recyclable material reduces the eco-contribution the brand pays per kilo, in every market where it sells, every year. This changes the pitch from a request for goodwill into a cost line. It also survives the objection that a small brand has no money for sustainability work, because the swap reduces an existing bill.

**Assumption.** The size of that saving for a realistic indie cosmetics volume has not been quantified. A brand shipping a few thousand units may see a saving too small to motivate action. This needs a worked example at real volumes before the argument is leaned on in the brief.

## 3. What the field already does

**Fact.** Carbonfact is fashion-specific, pulls from ERP and internal systems, works at SKU level, and runs what-if scenarios including material change, country of manufacture and transport method. It holds 150,000+ impact factors across 650+ material categories ([Carbonfact](https://www.carbonfact.com/methodology), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/29/carbonfact-is-a-carbon-management-platform-designed-specifically-for-the-fashion-industry/)).

**Fact.** Greenly is a broad climate and ESG platform for SMB and midmarket, automating product carbon footprints at scale via BOM imports and generating audit-ready GHG reports ([Greenly](https://greenly.earth/en-gb/blog/company-guide/understanding-a-product-carbon-footprint-pcf)).

**Fact.** Makersite targets large manufacturers and enterprises, modelling product cost, carbon, compliance and supply-chain risk together ([Zilch comparison](https://tryzilch.com/blog/best-product-carbon-footprint-software)).

**Fact.** Packaging converters offer design optimisation of their own. Ranpak has right-sizing software, Smurfit Westrock offers weight reduction and design support ([Pakoro](https://pakoro.com/blog/top-10-sustainable-packaging-companies-2026/)).

**Inference.** Three gaps show up consistently. The carbon platforms sell to companies with an ERP to integrate and a person whose job is sustainability, which an indie cosmetics brand has neither of. Their output is measurement and reporting, and where recommendation exists (Carbonfact's what-if) it is a modelling feature for the user to drive rather than a ranked recommendation filtered by what the user can actually do. The converters do recommend, and they recommend their own catalogue.

**Assumption, and the weakest claim in this pass.** No tool was found that combines constraint-filtered swap recommendations with independent supplier matching for small brands. A search failing to surface something is weaker evidence than verifying its absence. Before the brief claims a gap, the named competitors' actual product surfaces should be checked directly rather than through comparison pages, and the PPWR/EPR tool cluster (dpp-tool, ppwrcopilot, cubemos, Coolset, Tanso, PPWR Connect) should be checked for whether any has moved from documentation into recommendation.

## 4. The USP

**The statement.** Refractor recommends only the packaging changes a small brand can actually make: the same tooling, the same supplier wherever possible, no contact with the formula, no artwork redesign, and no increase in unit cost. Other tools calculate what a brand's footprint is. Refractor names what to change on the next purchase order.

**Inference: the differentiated asset is the filter, not the calculation.** The carbon arithmetic runs on free public emission factors and is reproducible by anyone. What is hard, and what nothing found in section 3 does for this segment, is knowing which of the possible swaps a ten-person brand can execute without new tooling, a new safety file, a repeat artwork cycle across three national labelling regimes, or a minimum order it cannot fund. A recommendation that ignores those constraints is discarded, which is why generic advice to "switch to sustainable packaging" does not move anything.

**Inference: the argument closes commercially because of eco-modulation.** The swaps that reduce carbon are largely the swaps that reduce EPR fees. Refractor can present a change as a reduction in an existing cost rather than a new expense. That is what makes it credible to a brand that is already struggling.

## 5. What makes a swap adoptable

Six gates. A candidate swap that fails any one of them should not reach the screen.

1. **Tooling.** The alternative runs in the existing mould or tool. No new tooling spend, no new lead time for tool manufacture.
2. **Formula contact.** The component does not touch the formula, so no compatibility or stability retesting and no PIF or CPSR update. This is why the MVP excludes the formula entirely and works on secondary and primary packaging only.
3. **Artwork.** No change to pack dimensions or decoration process. An artwork change is expensive out of proportion to its apparent size, because it re-triggers language, Triman, Italian material codes and sorting instructions across every market the brand sells in.
4. **Minimum order quantity.** Available at the volume the brand actually buys.
5. **Lead time.** Deliverable inside the brand's existing reorder cycle.
6. **Landed cost.** Neutral or better once the eco-modulated EPR fee change is included.

**Fact.** rPET and virgin PET are the same polymer, differing in feedstock, and post-consumer PET is reprocessed into pellets processed on the same equipment used for virgin PET ([SIPA](https://sipasolutions.com/blog/rpet-plastic-preforms-and-containers-problems-and-solutions), [Paramount Global](https://www.paramountglobal.com/knowledge/rpet-packaging-guide/)).

**Fact, and the necessary caveat.** A material change should not be judged on recycled-content percentage alone. The practical question is whether the new material delivers the required quality and forming window at production scale, so process validation is still required ([SIPA](https://sipasolutions.com/blog/rpet-plastic-preforms-and-containers-problems-and-solutions)). Custom bottle lead times run 4 to 8 weeks after mould approval ([Propacks](https://propacks.net/us/blog/packaging-lead-times-explained-from-sample-to-first-full-order)).

**Inference.** "No new tooling" is defensible for a virgin-to-recycled resin substitution. "No work at all" is not, and the product should say so. The honest claim is that the swap avoids tooling spend, artwork cycles and regulatory retesting, and still requires a production trial. Overstating this is the fastest way to lose a user on their first real attempt.

**Inference: the highest-value swaps are the ones that clear all six gates.** Virgin to recycled aluminium is the largest single reduction available (see section 8) and is a feedstock change. Virgin to recycled PET, HDPE and PP are the same shape of change. Removing a component outright, such as a secondary carton, a leaflet or a cellophane wrap, eliminates its entire contribution, changes no tooling, and reduces EPR fees by removing weight from the declaration.

## 6. When Refractor gets used

**Inference: the moment is the next packaging purchase order.** Packaging is bought in batches. Changing material between batches costs nothing extra, because the brand is placing an order regardless. Changing mid-batch means writing off unused inventory, which is why advice to change packaging fails when it arrives at an arbitrary moment. The reorder point is the only moment where a swap is close to free.

Secondary moments, in descending order of how cheap the change is:

- **New product development.** Nothing is locked, no tooling exists yet, no artwork has been approved. The cheapest possible intervention, and the least frequent.
- **Artwork refresh or rebrand.** The relabelling cost across national regimes is already being paid, so gate 3 falls away for the duration of that cycle.
- **Annual EPR reporting.** The brand is already assembling component materials and weights for its declarations. The data Refractor needs is in their hands at that moment and at no other.
- **A retailer or distributor asking for packaging documentation.** External pressure, arriving on someone else's schedule.

**Inference.** This makes Refractor a decision-support tool used at a purchasing moment. It is not a monitoring dashboard a brand visits regularly, and building it as one would misjudge the usage pattern. The product should expect infrequent, high-intent visits tied to a procurement or reporting event.

**Assumption.** Indie cosmetics reorder cadence is unverified. How often a small brand repurchases each packaging component determines how often Refractor is relevant to any single user, and therefore what retention could ever look like. Worth establishing before any success metric is set.

## 7. Turning a recommendation into an actual swap

The constraint is that Refractor takes no affiliate fee, no commission and no paid placement, which forecloses the mechanism most recommendation products use. Five levers remain, ranked by expected effect.

1. **Recommend the incumbent supplier first.** Where a brand's existing supplier already offers the alternative, switching cost is close to zero: no new account, no requalification, no new minimum order, no new relationship. This is both the highest-converting recommendation and the most honest one, and it is the recommendation a commission-funded tool has the least incentive to make.

2. **Reduce the action to sending one message.** Generate the actual request, pre-filled with the component specification, the target material, the brand's order volume and the recycled-content ask. The gap between an intention and a swap is usually the effort of composing a technical email the founder does not feel qualified to write.

3. **Lead with the fee reduction.** Present the eco-modulated EPR saving alongside the carbon delta, so the change reads as a reduction in an existing cost.

4. **Only show what is available at their volume.** A recommendation that fails on minimum order quantity teaches the user that the tool does not understand their business, and one such failure is enough to stop them returning.

5. **Ask, later, whether it happened.** With no transaction in the loop there is no other instrument.

**Inference, and an honest hole.** Refractor never observes conversion directly. It has no transaction, no commission and no visibility into the supplier relationship. Self-report at the next visit is the only available measurement, and self-report on infrequent visits is weak. This belongs in the success metrics section of the brief as a stated limitation rather than being hidden behind a proxy metric such as recommendations viewed.

**Assumption.** That a small brand's incumbent packaging supplier commonly offers a recycled-content version of the same component is the single load-bearing assumption behind lever 1. If incumbents rarely offer it, every recommendation becomes a supplier switch and the conversion argument weakens substantially. This should be checked against real supplier catalogues before the brief commits to it.

## 8. Emission factor sources

**Fact.** The UK Government GHG Conversion Factors, published 11 June 2026, are released under the Open Government Licence v3.0, which permits commercial reuse, as an Excel workbook and a flat file built for automatic processing ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/greenhouse-gas-reporting-conversion-factors-2026)).

**Fact.** DEFRA's material-use factors are given per tonne on an AR5-100 GWP basis, split virgin and recycled ([greencalculus packaging methodology](https://greencalculus.com/methodology/sector-pcf-packaging-methodology/)):

| Material | Virgin | Recycled | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| PET | 3,861.58 | 2,211.58 kg CO₂e/t | 43% |
| HDPE | 3,092.89 | 1,768.47 | 43% |
| LDPE/LLDPE | 2,963.59 | 1,095.58 | 63% |
| PP | 2,575.25 | 1,310.25 | 49% |
| Paper | 1,343.59 | 1,048.59 | 22% |
| Board | 1,198.24 | 1,096.63 | 8% |

**Fact.** Container glass and can-specific aluminium have no dedicated DEFRA factor. Aluminium figures come from the European Aluminium Environmental Profile Report, around 10.1 kg CO₂e/kg primary against 0.26 recycled ([greencalculus](https://greencalculus.com/methodology/sector-pcf-packaging-methodology/)).

**Fact, verified live 20 August 2026.** ADEME's Base Carbone is queryable at `https://data.ademe.fr/data-fair/api/v1/datasets/base-carboner/lines` with no API key, returns HTTP 200, carries 18,616 records under Licence Ouverte, exposes English name fields, and returns `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so a static browser-based prototype can call it directly. Queried values include new aluminium at 9,827 kg CO₂e/t against recycled aluminium at 513, and PP at 2,000.

**Fact.** Climatiq's free Starter tier carries a non-commercial licence, defined as any use "primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or monetary compensation". Data Pro is €2,000/year ([Climatiq pricing](https://www.climatiq.io/pricing)).

**Inference on architecture.** DEFRA bundled as the core factor table, version-pinned, with ADEME called live for the gaps DEFRA does not cover. A bundled table is deterministic, testable and unaffected by another service's availability. ADEME fills glass and aluminium and is free to call from the browser. Climatiq's non-commercial licence makes it a poor foundation for anything that might later take money.

**Fact, and a trap.** ADEME mixes production factors ("manufacture", per tonne) with end-of-life treatment factors (per tonne of waste), and several of the latter are negative, representing avoided burden. Queried PET and glass rows returned mostly the waste variety. Filtering by boundary before any value reaches the interface is required, or the product will show a component with negative emissions.

**Inference on scope of the claim.** DEFRA is UK-specific, runs roughly three years behind, and was built for company-level Scope 3 reporting rather than product LCA ([Lovat](https://secrreporting.co.uk/resources/defra-conversion-factors), [greencalculus](https://greencalculus.com/standards/uk-defra-emission-factors/)). This supports a directional comparison between two materials. It does not support a certified product footprint, and the product should present a delta between two options rather than an absolute footprint.

## 9. Open questions

Carried forward, plus what this pass raised.

1. Quantify the eco-modulated EPR saving for a realistic indie volume. If it is trivial at a few thousand units, section 2's commercial argument does not hold.
2. Do incumbent packaging suppliers commonly offer recycled-content versions of the same component? Section 7 lever 1 depends on it entirely.
3. Verify PPWR Articles 44, 45 and 68 against primary law once EUR-Lex serves the text.
4. Check the named competitors' actual product surfaces directly, and check whether any PPWR/EPR documentation tool has moved into recommendation.
5. Resolve the Spain and Netherlands PRO conflicts, and fill Croatia, Bulgaria, Romania, Switzerland and Liechtenstein.
6. Establish indie cosmetics packaging reorder cadence, which sets the ceiling on how often the product can be relevant.
7. Confirm whether individual large retailers impose carbon reporting through their own supplier programmes, beyond the general baseline in section 1.2.
8. Verify whether Annex I Part A of Regulation 1223/2009 requires full quantitative composition in the CPSR in those terms.
9. Monetization is deliberately open. The stated direction is that small brands are not charged, with public or institutional funding as a candidate. Nothing in this pass tests that.
