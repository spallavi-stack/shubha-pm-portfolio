# Refractor: Grounding Research

Internal backing material. Never rendered to HTML, never linked from the site. The product brief compresses this. This file keeps the full sourcing and confidence record so the depth stays reachable.

Every claim is tagged **Fact** (cited), **Inference** (reasoned, reasoning shown) or **Assumption** (unverified, flagged).

Scope: France, Italy, Germany, Spain and Poland. Second pass, 20 August 2026.

Not yet covered: pricing and willingness to pay, supplier catalogue reality at component level, personas.

## 1. Market: where the users are

**Fact.** Europe had 8,988 cosmetics manufacturing SMEs in 2023 across the EU-27 plus Norway, Switzerland and the UK. France held 1,917, Italy 952, Germany 880, Spain 766 and Poland 642 ([Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/579043/number-of-smes-in-the-cosmetic-industry-in-europe-by-country/)).

**Inference.** Those five countries hold 5,157 SMEs, 57% of the European base, and France alone holds 21%. Concentration this steep means a five-country scope reaches most of the addressable market while a ten-country scope would roughly double the regulatory research surface for the remaining long tail.

**Inference.** SME count is the right sizing metric here and market value is not. Europe's €110 billion cosmetics retail market is dominated by companies with their own regulatory departments, none of whom would use this product. Counting manufacturing SMEs counts organisations shaped like the target user.

**Fact.** Counting all registered cosmetics companies, France leads with 4,740 (11.8%), then the UK with 4,279 (10.7%), Germany 3,926, Spain 3,471 and Italy 3,427 ([companydata.com](https://companydata.com/europe/cosmetics-companies/)).

**Conflict, unresolved deliberately.** One market summary gives Germany at €18.0 billion and 21.6% share, and the UK at €14.7 billion and 17.4%, against a stated €110 billion European total. Those absolute figures work out to 16.4% and 13.4%, so the percentages sit against a different base or year ([market summary via search](https://cosmeticseurope.eu/our-industry/)). Nothing here depends on the value figures, and the primary Cosmetics Europe market performance report should be read before any of them is used.

**Assumption.** No source was found that counts indie or independent beauty brands specifically. SME manufacturing count is a proxy, and it includes contract manufacturers and ingredient suppliers who are not the target user. The real addressable number is smaller than 5,157 by an unknown margin.

## 2. The regulatory terrain

### 2.1 The product layer is harmonised and Refractor does not touch it

**Fact.** Regulation (EC) No 1223/2009 Article 11 requires the Responsible Person to keep a Product Information File for ten years after the last batch, containing a product description, the Cosmetic Product Safety Report under Article 10(1), the manufacturing method with a GMP compliance statement under Article 8, proof of claimed effect where justified, and animal testing data ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32009R1223)).

**Fact.** Article 10 requires a safety assessment before market placement, and Annex I Part B requires the assessor to hold a university qualification in pharmacy, toxicology, medicine or an equivalent discipline ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32009R1223)).

**Fact.** CPNP notification runs through one portal covering all 27 member states and only the Responsible Person can file it. The applicable GMP standard is EN ISO 22716:2007 ([Ecosistant](https://www.ecosistant.eu/en/the-responsible-person-role-under-the-cosmetics-regulation-1223-2009/)).

**Inference.** A brand does this once and can then sell anywhere in the EU. Brands plan and budget for this layer. Refractor operates entirely outside it, which is why the formula exclusion costs the product nothing.

### 2.2 Carbon is not what gates a retail listing

**Fact.** Retail buyers ask cosmetics brands for six categories of documentation before approving a listing: product safety documentation, Responsible Person information, claims substantiation files, ingredient and formula documentation, testing and stability records, and packaging and labelling compliance review ([GCRS, June 2026](https://www.gcrs.co.uk/the-compliance-documentation-retail-buyers-expect-before-approving-cosmetic-listings/)). Carbon footprint appears in none of them. The only environmental foothold is "sustainable" as one claim type inside claims substantiation.

**Inference.** Refractor should not claim to clear a listing gate, because there is no carbon gate to clear. The source is a consultancy selling retail-readiness services, so it has every incentive to list each document a brand might need. Absence from a maximal list carries more weight than absence from a minimal one.

**Assumption.** Individual large retailers may impose carbon reporting through their own supplier programmes. Unverified, and a single major retailer mandating it would change the picture.

### 2.3 The packaging layer is fragmented, and that is the whole opportunity

**Fact.** There is no EU-wide packaging registration. A producer registers, reports and pays in every member state where its packaging reaches a customer ([ppwrobligations.com](https://ppwrobligations.com/epr/), [ecocomply](https://ecocomply.ai/blog/packaging-epr-registration-country-by-country)).

| | Register and PRO | Threshold | National packaging mark | Label language |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **France** | Citeo or Léko | Not established | Triman logo plus French sorting instructions | French, full translation by law |
| **Italy** | CONAI, six material consortia | Not established | Material code on every component, Italian sorting instructions | Italian, full translation by law |
| **Germany** | LUCID (ZSVR) plus dual system contract | None | None | German |
| **Spain** | Ecoembes for light packaging, Ecovidrio for glass | Not established | Disposal fraction indication | Spanish |
| **Poland** | Rekopol, moving to NFOŚiGW from August 2026 | None, micro-enterprises included | None | Polish |

Sources: [Compliance Gate](https://www.compliancegate.com/epr-organisations-european-union/), [Eldris](https://epr.eldris.ai/data-centre/eu-regulation/epr-packaging-regulations-eu-guide-by-country-2025/), [Biorius on language](https://biorius.com/cosmetic-news/eu-cosmetic-language-requirements/), [Lizenzero on Triman](https://www.lizenzero.de/en/blog/labelling-requirements-in-france-the-triman-logo-and-sorting-instructions/), [UL Solutions on Italy](https://www.ul.com/news/italy-environmental-labeling-requirements-packaging), [Ecoveritas on Spain](https://www.ecoveritas.com/articles/mandatory-environmental-labelling-for-packaging-across-europe-what-you-need-to-know).

**Fact.** Italy's material identification codes (PET 1, PP 5, PAP 21, GL 70, ALU 41) apply to every packaging component, with fines of €5,200 to €40,000 for missing or incorrect marking ([Valpak](https://www.valpak.co.uk/introduction-of-environmental-labelling-requirement-in-italy/)).

**Conflict, unresolved deliberately.** Spain's PROs appear as Ecoembes and Ecovidrio in one source and as Procircular and ECOTIC in another. One source also lists Erion for Italy, which is a WEEE scheme, so that source is treated as unreliable throughout. Both need checking against national registers before anything public quotes them.

**Fact.** France requires manufacturers and distributors established there to file an establishment declaration, importers exempt, with DGCCRF taking this over from ANSM on 1 January 2024 ([coslaw.eu](https://coslaw.eu/france-national-requirements-for-cosmetic-products/)). Italy requires Ministry of Health notification if manufacturing or packaging in Italy ([Lexora](https://lexoraeu.com/cosmetic-compliance-italy/)). Germany requires the Responsible Person to notify local authorities of importer status where Germany is the first EU country of import ([coslaw.eu](https://coslaw.eu/national-requirements-to-place-cosmetics-on-the-german-market/)).

**Inference.** The EU harmonised the product and left the packaging to member states. Every national divergence that costs a brand real money is a packaging divergence: a mark on the pack, a language on the pack, a register that wants component materials and weights, a fee priced per kilo per material. A brand selling into all five supplies the same component-level data five times in five formats.

**Excluded and why.** The UK ranks second by company count and is out of scope. Post-Brexit it runs a parallel stack with its own Responsible Person, its own SCPN notification portal, UK RP details on the label since 31 December 2022, and separate packaging EPR ([Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/news/the-uk-regulation-the-cosmetic-regulation-after-brexit/)). One market costing a duplicate of every requirement is a poor trade at this stage.

### 2.4 PPWR sits on top

**Fact.** Regulation (EU) 2025/40 became generally applicable on **12 August 2026**. From that date no packaging goes on the EU market without a valid EU Declaration of Conformity, with no transition or sell-through period for existing stock ([Circularise](https://www.circularise.com/blogs/ppwr-guide-to-compliance-timelines-and-mass-balance-solutions), [Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity)).

**Fact.** Anyone placing packaged goods on the market under their own brand, having determined the packaging design, is the manufacturer for this purpose and signs the DoC with full legal responsibility. The technical documentation behind it needs material and resin composition, recycled content percentages, empty-space ratios and heavy metal documentation ([Coolset](https://www.coolset.com/academy/ppwr-declaration-of-conformity), [Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/news/eu-ppwr-cosmetic-packaging-compliance/)).

**Fact.** Article 16, read directly from the regulation: "Suppliers of packaging or packaging materials provide the manufacturer with all the information and documentation necessary for the manufacturer to demonstrate the conformity of the packaging and the packaging materials" ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202500040)).

**Fact.** From 1 January 2030 design-for-recycling compliance applies, plastic packaging must carry a minimum 35% post-consumer recycled content, and packaging graded below E-level (under 70% recyclable) is prohibited ([Cosmeservice](https://cosmeservice.com/news/eu-ppwr-cosmetic-packaging-compliance/)).

**Fact.** Micro-enterprise relief is narrow. Under 10 employees with turnover or balance sheet at or below €2 million lifts the technical documentation and DoC obligation only where the packaging supplier is EU-based in the same member state. No relief from EPR ([Packaging Hub](https://packaginghub.fr/en/articles/ppwr-micro-enterprises)).

**Assumption, flagged.** Several secondary sources state that Article 44 requires registration in each member state's producer register, that Article 45 requires a non-established producer to appoint an Authorised Representative in each state where it makes packaging available from 12 August 2026, and that Article 68 requires penalty rules by 12 February 2027. EUR-Lex returned HTTP 202 on three URL forms during this pass, so these are unverified against primary law.

**Inference.** Article 16 settles the question of whether packaging data is obtainable. A brand has a legal right to demand material composition, recycled content and weights from its suppliers. What remains is knowing what to ask for and tracking who answered.

**Inference.** The 2030 recycled content threshold turns every swap Refractor recommends into work a brand has to do eventually. A recommendation to move a component to recycled feedstock is a pull-forward of a mandatory change, which is a stronger position than asking for a discretionary one.

## 3. Eco-modulation: why a swap pays for itself

This is what makes a free tool viable for a brand with no sustainability budget.

**Fact.** CONAI has modulated its packaging fee since 2018 for plastic and 2019 for paper, structured to promote higher reusability and recyclability ([CONAI](https://www.conai.org/en/companies/services-for-managing-consortium-obligations-and-business-opportunities/epr-fee/modulated-epr-fee-for-plastic-packaging/)).

**Fact.** Under PPWR, eco-modulated fees tied to recyclability grades become mandatory in every national scheme on bonus and malus logic. Packaging that is easier to collect, sort and recycle attracts lower fees ([Tanso](https://www.tanso.de/en/blog/epr-under-the-ppwr-extended-producer-responsibility-modulated-fees-and-registration-in-the-lucid-register)).

**Fact, weakly sourced.** Citeo fees are reported at roughly €100 to €700 per tonne with surcharges up to 50% for hard-to-sort packaging ([RegSurance](https://regsurance.com/eco-modulation-ppwr-epr-packaging-fees/)). RegSurance sells fee-reduction services, so it has an interest in the spread looking wide. Verify against Citeo's published tariff schedule before this figure goes anywhere public.

**Inference.** A recommended swap returns money. Moving a component to recycled content or a more recyclable material lowers the eco-contribution per kilo, in every market the brand sells into, every year. The pitch becomes a reduction in a bill the brand already pays. That answers the objection that a small brand has no budget for sustainability work.

**Assumption, and the one that decides whether section 3 holds.** Nobody has quantified this saving at indie volumes. A brand shipping a few thousand units may see a number too small to act on. A worked example at real volumes across the five markets is needed before the brief leans on this.

## 4. What the field already does

**Fact.** Carbonfact is fashion-specific, pulls from ERP and internal systems, works at SKU level and runs what-if scenarios covering material change, country of manufacture and transport. It holds over 150,000 impact factors across 650+ material categories ([Carbonfact](https://www.carbonfact.com/methodology)).

**Fact.** Greenly is a broad climate and ESG platform for SMB and midmarket, automating product carbon footprints via BOM imports and generating audit-ready GHG reports ([Greenly](https://greenly.earth/en-gb/blog/company-guide/understanding-a-product-carbon-footprint-pcf)).

**Fact.** Makersite targets large manufacturers, modelling product cost, carbon, compliance and supply-chain risk together ([Zilch](https://tryzilch.com/blog/best-product-carbon-footprint-software)).

**Fact.** Packaging converters offer their own design optimisation. Ranpak has right-sizing software and Smurfit Westrock offers weight reduction support ([Pakoro](https://pakoro.com/blog/top-10-sustainable-packaging-companies-2026/)).

**Inference.** Three patterns hold across the carbon platforms. They sell to companies with an ERP to integrate and a person whose job is sustainability. Their output is measurement and reporting. Where recommendation exists, as in Carbonfact's what-if scenarios, it is a modelling surface the user drives themselves. The converters do recommend, and they recommend their own catalogue.

**Assumption, and the weakest claim in this document.** No tool was found combining constraint-filtered swap recommendations with independent supplier matching for small brands. A search that fails to surface something is weak evidence of its absence. Before the brief claims this gap, the named competitors' actual product surfaces need checking directly, and the PPWR tooling cluster (dpp-tool, ppwrcopilot, cubemos, Coolset, Tanso, PPWR Connect) needs checking for whether any has moved from documentation into recommendation.

## 5. The USP

**The statement.** Refractor recommends only the packaging changes a small brand can actually make: same tooling, same supplier where possible, no contact with the formula, no artwork redesign, no increase in unit cost. Other tools calculate what a brand's footprint is. Refractor names what to change on the next purchase order.

**Inference.** The differentiated asset is the filter. Carbon arithmetic runs on free public emission factors and anyone can reproduce it. The hard part is knowing which of the possible swaps a ten-person brand can execute without new tooling, a new safety file, a repeat artwork cycle across three national labelling regimes, or a minimum order it cannot fund. Recommendations that ignore those constraints get discarded, which is why advice to switch to sustainable packaging moves nothing.

**Inference.** Eco-modulation closes the argument commercially. The swaps that cut carbon are largely the swaps that cut EPR fees, so a change can be presented as a reduction in an existing cost.

**Caveat inherited from section 4.** This positioning depends on no existing tool doing the same thing for this segment, and that claim is tagged Assumption above. The USP is provisional until the competitor check is done.

## 6. What makes a swap adoptable

Six gates. A candidate failing any one of them should not reach the screen.

1. **Tooling.** Runs in the existing mould. No tooling spend, no tool manufacture lead time.
2. **Formula contact.** Does not touch the formula, so no compatibility or stability retesting and no PIF or CPSR update.
3. **Artwork.** No change to pack dimensions or decoration process.
4. **Minimum order quantity.** Available at the volume the brand actually buys.
5. **Lead time.** Deliverable inside the existing reorder cycle.
6. **Landed cost.** Neutral or better once the eco-modulated fee change is counted.

**Inference on gate 3.** An artwork change costs more than its apparent size because it re-triggers work across every market the brand sells into. Section 2.3 establishes that France, Italy and Spain each mandate a different national mark and each of the five requires its own language. A dimension change touches all of it at once. This reasoning is sound and the cost itself is unquantified.

**Fact.** rPET and virgin PET are the same polymer differing in feedstock, and post-consumer PET is reprocessed into pellets run on the same equipment used for virgin PET ([SIPA](https://sipasolutions.com/blog/rpet-plastic-preforms-and-containers-problems-and-solutions), [Paramount Global](https://www.paramountglobal.com/knowledge/rpet-packaging-guide/)).

**Fact.** A material change should not be judged on recycled content percentage alone. The practical question is whether the material delivers the required quality and forming window at production scale, so process validation is still required ([SIPA](https://sipasolutions.com/blog/rpet-plastic-preforms-and-containers-problems-and-solutions)). Custom bottle lead times run 4 to 8 weeks after mould approval ([Propacks](https://propacks.net/us/blog/packaging-lead-times-explained-from-sample-to-first-full-order)).

**Inference.** "No new tooling" holds for a virgin-to-recycled resin substitution. "No work at all" does not, and the product should say so. The swap avoids tooling spend, artwork cycles and regulatory retesting, and it still needs a production trial. Overstating this loses a user on their first real attempt.

**Inference on which swaps matter most.** Virgin to recycled aluminium is the largest single reduction available and it is a feedstock change. PET, HDPE and PP follow the same shape. Removing a component outright, a secondary carton or a leaflet or a cellophane wrap, eliminates its entire contribution, changes no tooling, and cuts EPR fees by taking weight off the declaration.

## 7. When Refractor gets used

**Inference.** The moment is the next packaging purchase order. Packaging is bought in batches, so changing material between batches costs nothing extra because the brand is placing an order anyway. Changing mid-batch means writing off inventory. The reorder point is the only moment a swap is close to free, and it explains why advice arriving at an arbitrary moment gets ignored.

Secondary moments, cheapest first:

- **New product development.** Nothing locked, no tooling, no approved artwork. Cheapest possible intervention and the least frequent.
- **Artwork refresh or rebrand.** The relabelling cost is already being paid, so gate 3 falls away for that cycle.
- **Annual EPR reporting.** The brand is already assembling component materials and weights. The data Refractor needs is in their hands at that moment and at no other.
- **A retailer or distributor asking for packaging documentation.** External pressure on someone else's schedule.

**Inference.** This makes Refractor a decision-support tool used at a purchasing moment. Building it as a monitoring dashboard would misjudge the usage pattern. Expect infrequent, high-intent visits tied to a procurement or reporting event.

**Assumption.** Indie cosmetics reorder cadence is unverified, and it sets the ceiling on how often the product can be relevant to any one user. Establish this before any retention metric is written.

## 8. Turning a recommendation into a swap

No affiliate fee, no commission, no paid placement. That forecloses the mechanism most recommendation products use. Five levers remain, ranked by expected effect.

1. **Recommend the incumbent supplier first.** Where the brand's existing supplier already offers the alternative, switching cost is close to zero: no new account, no requalification, no new minimum order. It is the highest-converting recommendation and also the most honest one, and it is the recommendation a commission-funded tool has least incentive to make.

2. **Reduce the action to sending one message.** Generate the request pre-filled with the component specification, target material, order volume and recycled content ask.

3. **Lead with the fee reduction.** Show the eco-modulated saving alongside the carbon delta so the change reads as a cost line.

4. **Show only what is available at their volume.** A recommendation that fails on minimum order quantity teaches the user the tool does not understand their business, and one failure is enough to stop them coming back.

5. **Ask later whether it happened.** With no transaction in the loop there is no other instrument.

**Inference, and an honest hole.** Refractor never observes conversion. No transaction, no commission, no visibility into the supplier relationship. Self-report at the next visit is the only measurement available, and self-report across infrequent visits is weak. This belongs in the brief's success metrics as a stated limitation, and it should not be replaced with a proxy such as recommendations viewed.

**Assumption.** Lever 1 rests entirely on incumbent suppliers commonly offering recycled-content versions of the same component. If they rarely do, every recommendation becomes a supplier switch and the conversion argument weakens badly. Check against real supplier catalogues before the brief commits.

## 9. Emission factor sources

**Fact.** The UK Government GHG Conversion Factors, published 11 June 2026, are released under the Open Government Licence v3.0, which permits commercial reuse, as an Excel workbook and a flat file built for automatic processing ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/greenhouse-gas-reporting-conversion-factors-2026)).

**Fact.** DEFRA material-use factors, per tonne, AR5-100 GWP, split virgin and recycled ([greencalculus](https://greencalculus.com/methodology/sector-pcf-packaging-methodology/)):

| Material | Virgin | Recycled | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| PET | 3,861.58 | 2,211.58 kg CO₂e/t | 43% |
| HDPE | 3,092.89 | 1,768.47 | 43% |
| LDPE/LLDPE | 2,963.59 | 1,095.58 | 63% |
| PP | 2,575.25 | 1,310.25 | 49% |
| Paper | 1,343.59 | 1,048.59 | 22% |
| Board | 1,198.24 | 1,096.63 | 8% |

**Fact.** Container glass and can-specific aluminium have no dedicated DEFRA factor. Aluminium comes from the European Aluminium Environmental Profile Report at roughly 10.1 kg CO₂e/kg primary against 0.26 recycled ([greencalculus](https://greencalculus.com/methodology/sector-pcf-packaging-methodology/)).

**Fact, verified live on 20 August 2026.** ADEME's Base Carbone is queryable at `https://data.ademe.fr/data-fair/api/v1/datasets/base-carboner/lines` with no API key. It returns HTTP 200, carries 18,616 records under Licence Ouverte, exposes English name fields and returns `access-control-allow-origin: *`, so a static browser prototype can call it directly. Queried values include new aluminium at 9,827 kg CO₂e/t against recycled aluminium at 513, and PP at 2,000.

**Fact.** Climatiq's free Starter tier carries a non-commercial licence covering any use "primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or monetary compensation". Data Pro is €2,000 a year ([Climatiq](https://www.climatiq.io/pricing)).

**Inference on architecture.** Bundle DEFRA as the core factor table, version-pinned, and call ADEME live for the gaps. A bundled table is deterministic, testable and unaffected by another service's uptime. ADEME covers glass and aluminium and costs nothing to call from the browser. Climatiq's licence makes it a poor foundation for anything that might later take money. That France is both the largest target market and the source of the free open API is a convenient alignment.

**Fact, and a trap.** ADEME mixes production factors ("manufacture", per tonne) with end-of-life treatment factors (per tonne of waste), several of the latter negative to represent avoided burden. Queried PET and glass rows returned mostly the waste variety. Filter by boundary before any value reaches the interface, or the product will show a jar with negative emissions.

**Inference on the scope of the claim.** DEFRA is UK-specific, runs about three years behind and was built for company-level Scope 3 reporting ([greencalculus](https://greencalculus.com/standards/uk-defra-emission-factors/)). It supports a directional comparison between two materials. It does not support a certified product footprint, which is why the product compares options and stops there.
