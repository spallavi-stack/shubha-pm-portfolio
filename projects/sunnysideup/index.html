Problem & positioning

The market signal

  • The CMA's 2022 investigation into the solar sector specifically flagged "inaccurate and unrealistic headline price information."
  • A Which? survey found more than a third of solar-panel owners had been cold-called with false claims, including bogus "government-required" checks.
  • Every UK solar comparison platform identified in research (CompareMySolar, Uswitch, Solar Selections, Easy PV, Renewable Energy Hub, Solar Planet, Solar PV Quotes Compare) runs a lead-generation or referral-fee model.
  • BS 7671 Amendment 4 (in force 15 April 2026) legalized electrician-installed plug-in solar; true DIY self-install becomes legal 27 August 2026 under SI 2026/848.
  • 2025 was UK solar's record installation year, 31-37% growth, so rooftop demand, and the sales pressure that comes with it, is already at an all-time high.

Positioning

SunnySideUp doesn't sell, install, or take referral fees from installers. It's an independent viability check used at two moments: before any sales conversation, to set a baseline, or after receiving a quote, to get an unbiased second opinion. The closest real analog is Energy Saving Trust, a government/private-funded impartial-advice body, but even it defers the final personalized number back to the installer being evaluated. SunnySideUp gives that specific, personalized read directly.

What synthetic interviews surfaced

All three personas were interviewed with the same guide (AI-generated, in-character, grounded strictly in each persona's brief and real UK research, explicitly flagged as synthetic, not real user data). Cross-persona themes directly shaped the roadmap:

01

Trust comes from independence plus honesty about limits

All three trust conditions center on non-referral independence, and, for Aisha, a willingness to say "this part is genuinely unclear."

02

A "red" result is trusted and acted on

All three would accept an unfavorable result and change their behavior, specifically because of the non-referral positioning.

03

Sharing is peer-to-peer

All three said they'd tell someone specific, a neighbour, a sister, a renters' Facebook group, rather than post publicly.

04

Aisha's disappointment is capped, and no product design fixes that

Her ceiling is structurally lower than the rooftop segment's for as long as the tenancy-consent question stays legally unresolved, independent of what SunnySideUp itself does well.

A prioritization call worth showing

The plug-in calculator's Confidence score (6) is deliberately the lowest in Now. Aisha's segment is the newly-legal, most differentiated part of the pitch, but grounding-research.md itself flags plug-in cost and generation figures as its weakest-sourced section, and that caveat ships inside the tool's own output to real users, carried through to launch rather than dropped along the way.

Limitations

Industry commentary on AI-assisted prototypes in 2025-2026 converges on one point: a polished demo and a shippable product are different things, and it's easy to mistake one for the other. A few of the gaps commonly named by product practitioners, applied honestly to SunnySideUp:

A working demo isn't 10% of the way to done

  • The calculator genuinely live-fetches three real sources: a postcode's region and coordinates from postcodes.io, solar irradiance history from Open-Meteo, and current electricity prices from Octopus Energy's public API, each tagged with its own sourcing tier in the code.
  • What's still missing is everything around that calculation: no real backend, no account or data persistence, and no quote-upload parsing (that stays a self-reported, manual-entry comparison for now, and is already scoped as a Next-horizon, post-validation roadmap item rather than an MVP gap).
  • The underlying payback and generation figures are wide, industry-consensus ranges corroborated across multiple sources; no single authoritative UK payback dataset exists to draw a tighter number from.
Source: Kyros, "From Prototype to Production"

Automated design still needs real users

  • AI-generated screens can miss subtle hierarchy, spacing, and comprehension issues that only surface with real usability testing.
  • SunnySideUp's personas and interviews are synthetic and internally consistent, but were never tested against an actual homeowner, renter, or leaseholder clicking through the flow.
Source: Parallel, "AI-Powered Prototyping for UI/UX Design"

Some edge cases are handled, most aren't

  • AI-built prototypes typically skip error states and unusual inputs entirely. This one is a partial exception: an unrecognized postcode, a failed lookup, or an unexpected API error each get a specific message and a graceful fallback to an England-calibrated default, rather than a silent break.
  • What's still unhandled: an unusually shaped roof, conflicting self-reported values (e.g. a stated consumption figure that doesn't match the household-size estimate), and any quote-upload edge cases, since that feature doesn't exist yet.
Source: CodeConductor, "Vibe Coding for PMs"

Prototype code isn't production code

  • AI-generated prototypes are typically isolated from any real codebase and would need to be re-implemented by an engineering team to actually ship.
  • The prototype is static HTML/CSS/JS with no data layer, no security review, and no compliance pass, relevant since a real version would handle a user's address, energy usage, and quote details.
Source: monday.com, "Vibe coding for product managers"
